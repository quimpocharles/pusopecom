# PusoStore Journey Maps

**Version 1.0**

The Information Architecture describes the structure a fan moves through. This document describes the fan — thirteen situations a real person arrives in, each carrying a different emotional state, a different amount of patience, and a different bar for what "trustworthy" needs to mean in the moment. The same Product page serves all thirteen. It cannot serve them the same way.

Each journey is mapped across seven dimensions: the emotional state a fan actually carries into it, their goals, what trust has to be true for them to proceed, the real decision points along the way, where the platform earns commerce from getting it right, where it typically breaks, and how it recovers when it does.

This builds directly on the **Domain Model**, **Capability Model**, and **Information Architecture** — journeys are described in terms of the concepts and structure already defined there (Favorite, Wishlist, Drop, Trust, Confidence, Discovery, contextual commerce), not reinvented from scratch.

---

## First Visit

**Emotional state.** Curious but guarded. Arrives with no established relationship to the platform, carrying the market's baseline skepticism about buying sports merchandise online — this fan has likely encountered counterfeit or unreliable sellers before and isn't yet convinced this is different.

**Goals.** Figure out fast whether this is legitimate, and whether it's actually for "my" team. Rarely fully undecided — most first visits arrive already knowing which Organization they care about.

**Trust requirements.** A visible Trust & Verification signal within the first moments matters more here than anywhere else in this map, because this fan has no track record with the platform to fall back on yet.

**Decision points.** Explore Discovery or search directly for their team. Create an account or browse anonymously. Whether the first Organization or Product they see feels authentic enough to keep going.

**Commerce opportunities.** A well-chosen Story or Campaign converting curiosity into a Favorite — even before any purchase — is the highest-leverage moment to capture this fan, since a Favorite established now shapes every future Discovery visit.

**Failure points.** A generic, product-grid-first presentation that reads as "just another marketplace." No visible verification. Being routed into a category browse instead of straight to the Organization actually searched for.

**Recovery opportunities.** An unmissable Trust signal on the very first Organization or Product viewed. Search correctly routing an identity-bearing query straight to the right Organization even on an imperfect first attempt.

## Returning Fan

**Emotional state.** Comfortable and purpose-driven. Already trusts the platform in principle, arriving with an established relationship — Favorites already set — rather than needing to be convinced of anything.

**Goals.** Check on "their" Organization(s): new Drops, Campaigns, Stories. Often no specific purchase in mind yet.

**Trust requirements.** Ongoing, not initial. This fan is watching whether trust already extended is being honored — did the last order arrive as promised, is pricing consistent — more than looking for a fresh signal.

**Decision points.** Go straight to a Favorited Organization, or linger in Discovery. Whether a new Drop or Campaign is worth acting on now versus later.

**Commerce opportunities.** Personalized Discovery surfacing something genuinely new from a Favorited Organization is the single highest-conversion moment on the platform — intent and trust are both already established, so this fan needs less persuading than any other.

**Failure points.** Discovery treating this fan the same as an anonymous first-time visitor. Nothing new from their Organizations since the last visit, making the return feel wasted.

**Recovery opportunities.** Even absent a new Drop, surfacing a Story or heritage content from a Favorited Organization keeps the visit from feeling empty and keeps the relationship warm between commercial moments.

## Buying Jersey

**Emotional state.** Decisive and specific. Motivated by identity, not comparison-shopping — this purchase is a public declaration of belonging, not a considered retail decision.

**Goals.** Confirm size and authenticity quickly, then complete with minimal friction. Speed matters because the emotional trigger — a call-up, a big match, a gift occasion — is often time-bound.

**Trust requirements.** Size and fit confidence, honestly scored, and delivery-timing confidence — this fan often needs the item by a specific date and needs to know that's realistic before committing.

**Decision points.** Which size or variant. Whether to trust an AI-assisted size recommendation or go with instinct instead. Whether to add anything else from the same Organization while already in a buying mindset.

**Commerce opportunities.** Cross-linking to the same Organization's other Collections while purchase intent is highest. Surfacing a related Story that deepens the purchase's meaning rather than just its price.

**Failure points.** An overconfident, wrong size recommendation, which erodes trust in both this purchase and every future one. Unclear delivery timing against a date that mattered to the purchase in the first place.

**Recovery opportunities.** Honest Confidence scoring that undersells rather than oversells certainty. Proactive delivery-timing communication the moment a delay risk is known — not after the date has already passed.

## Watching Broadcast

**Emotional state.** Immersed and reactive. Not in a shopping mindset at all until a specific in-broadcast moment — a highlight, a player's gear, a big win — creates a sudden, short-lived spike of purchase intent.

**Goals.** Act on the moment while the feeling is still live. This journey has the shortest patience in the whole map, because the trigger decays fast once the broadcast moves on.

**Trust requirements.** Instant credibility. There's no time here for independent verification — the contextual commerce link has to carry the platform's trust by association with the broadcast the fan already trusts.

**Decision points.** Tap through now, or lose the moment. There is effectively no "browse and decide later" branch without losing most of the intent that created it.

**Commerce opportunities.** This is the journey that most directly cashes in the platform's structural advantage — no competitor without broadcast ownership can build this moment at all. A direct, one-tap path from the moment to the exact item is the entire opportunity.

**Failure points.** Any delay or extra step between the broadcast moment and the Product. Landing on a generic Product page that's lost the context of why the fan tapped through.

**Recovery opportunities.** Preserving the contextual pointer back to the moment even if the fan doesn't convert immediately, so a Wishlist save still carries the "why" and can be revisited without the moment feeling permanently lost.

## Finding Team

**Emotional state.** Mildly determined, sometimes uncertain. Often a new fan, sometimes prompted by a friend or family member — knows roughly who they're looking for but isn't sure the platform will actually carry "a small team" or "my barangay league."

**Goals.** Confirm the Organization exists at all, and that it's genuinely theirs, before investing more effort.

**Trust requirements.** Confirmation that even a small, local Organization gets the same legitimacy treatment as a national team — this fan is implicitly testing whether the platform serves the whole pyramid or only its top.

**Decision points.** Search by name versus browse by league or sport facet. Whether an initial no-result moment reads as "not here" or as motivation to try a different term.

**Commerce opportunities.** A small Organization's first-ever fan finding them here — proof the platform serves the grassroots layer, not just the professional one — is disproportionately important to the platform's broader trust, even when the individual transaction is small.

**Failure points.** A search or facet structure that quietly favors large, well-known Organizations. A thin or unfinished profile reading as illegitimate rather than simply new.

**Recovery opportunities.** Search returning a partial or fuzzy match rather than a hard zero. A minimum standard of presentation — Organization identity and Trust status visible — applied uniformly regardless of size.

## Searching Player

**Emotional state.** Personally invested, often more attached to the individual than to any team — matching the pattern where individual-athlete fandom in the Philippines regularly exceeds team or league attachment.

**Goals.** Find gear connected specifically to one person, not their current club or federation generically.

**Trust requirements.** Confirmation the Athlete's presence is genuinely theirs or run with their authorization, rather than an unofficial tribute page — a direct hit to the platform's core trust claim if it isn't.

**Decision points.** Search by athlete name versus navigate through whichever Organization they currently represent. Whether to also Favorite the Team they play for, extending the relationship beyond the individual.

**Commerce opportunities.** Cross-linking an Athlete to every Organization or Team they've ever represented turns a single-purpose visit into a much broader discovery session across that athlete's whole career.

**Failure points.** An athlete-name search returning only products that happen to mention them in a description, rather than the Athlete's own identity. An athlete with no independent presence at all, forcing the fan into an unrelated club's full catalog.

**Recovery opportunities.** Search treating Athlete as a first-class result type exactly as Organization is. A clear invitation to Favorite the Athlete directly, distinct from Favoriting any Team they happen to represent.

## International Fan

**Emotional state.** Interested but distant. Engaging with Philippine sports from outside the country without the built-in cultural trust a domestic fan extends by default — may be discovering an Organization for the first time rather than carrying lifelong loyalty.

**Goals.** Confirm the platform will actually deliver to them, at a real and predictable cost, before investing further interest.

**Trust requirements.** Transparent international shipping cost and timeline before checkout, not revealed as a late surprise — for a fan with no existing relationship to fall back on, a bad surprise here is close to unrecoverable.

**Decision points.** Proceed once landed cost — item plus shipping plus any duties — is known. Whether the wait time is acceptable for what's often a considered purchase rather than an identity-affirming impulse buy.

**Commerce opportunities.** An international fan converting is disproportionately valuable evidence that the platform's reach extends beyond the Philippines — worth treating as strategically important even where per-order economics look thinner than a domestic Drop.

**Failure points.** Shipping cost or delivery estimate only surfacing at the final checkout step. No clarity on customs or duties responsibility, a common abandonment trigger for cross-border purchases generally.

**Recovery opportunities.** Surfacing shipping cost and timeline as early as the Product page, not held back until checkout. Clear, plain communication of what happens with customs.

## OFW

**Emotional state.** Homesick pride. Distinct from the general International Fan — this fan's relationship to Philippine sports is inseparable from their relationship to home itself. A purchase here is often an act of representing or reconnecting, since a purchase is frequently the only available act of fandom for someone who can't attend a game in person.

**Goals.** Two goals often present at once: buying something to wear themselves, as a stand-in for presence at home, and buying something to send home as a gift — continuing a tradition, the balikbayan box, that already exists independent of this platform.

**Trust requirements.** The same authenticity trust every fan needs, plus specific confidence that a gift sent home will actually arrive intact and on time. A failure here doesn't just lose a sale — it damages a moment this fan was building toward for someone else, which carries far more weight than a self-purchase gone wrong.

**Decision points.** Ship to self abroad, ship directly to family in the Philippines, or route through an existing balikbayan consolidation channel. Timing a purchase around a visit home, a remittance cycle, or a specific occasion.

**Commerce opportunities.** The segment identified as potentially larger, in commercial terms, than the domestic base — the same pattern Real Madrid and Manchester United discovered in their own diaspora. A first-class "send to the Philippines" flow, treated as a real delivery pattern rather than an edge case, is a structural advantage no foreign or domestic-only competitor can casually replicate.

**Failure points.** Being treated identically to a domestic Customer, with no accommodation for the two-destination reality of this fan's life. A gift shipment arriving late for the occasion it was bought for — a broken promise to someone this fan couldn't be there for in person.

**Recovery opportunities.** A delivery option explicitly designed around gifting-to-the-Philippines-from-abroad, communicated clearly enough that this fan doesn't have to guess whether it's supported. Proactive Notifications on a gift shipment specifically, since this fan can't personally verify anything happened correctly on the ground.

## Gift Purchase

**Emotional state.** Slightly anxious generosity. Buying for someone else's identity, not their own, carries the risk of getting someone else's fandom wrong — wrong team, wrong size, wrong player.

**Goals.** Get it right for the recipient, ideally with enough of a safety net — sizing flexibility, presentation, timing — that a guess doesn't become an obviously wrong one.

**Trust requirements.** Confidence the item will read as genuinely thoughtful and authentic to the recipient, not generic. Authenticity here isn't just about the seller — it's about whether the gift actually reflects the recipient's specific fandom.

**Decision points.** Whose team to buy for, if uncertain. What size to choose without the recipient present to confirm. Ship directly to the recipient or to the giver first for personal delivery.

**Commerce opportunities.** A Wishlist a recipient has already built, where they're also a platform Customer, turns an uncertain guess into a confident, specific purchase — one of the strongest arguments for Wishlist mattering beyond the person who built it.

**Failure points.** No way to check a recipient's saved preferences, forcing a blind guess. A generic delivery experience that doesn't read as a gift at all by the time it arrives.

**Recovery opportunities.** A gift-purchase path surfacing a recipient's Wishlist where one exists and permission allows. Sizing guidance framed for a gift-giver specifically, rather than assuming the buyer is the wearer.

## Limited Drop

**Emotional state.** High urgency, borderline anxious excitement. Scarcity and a countdown convert ordinary interest into real pressure — precisely the intensity that infrastructure most needs to hold, not the moment to be tested for the first time.

**Goals.** Get the item before it sells out, at the size or variant wanted, without the process itself becoming the story instead of the product.

**Trust requirements.** Confidence that Inventory is accurate in real time, and that "sold out" means genuinely sold out. Nothing damages trust faster in this journey than completing a purchase only to be told afterward that stock wasn't actually available.

**Decision points.** Wait for the Drop to go live, or set a reminder and hope not to miss it. Which variant to prioritize if the first choice sells out first. How long to keep trying before giving up.

**Commerce opportunities.** A well-run Drop is the platform's single highest-intensity conversion moment and, handled honestly, becomes trust-building evidence in itself — proof the platform can be relied on exactly when it matters most.

**Failure points.** Overselling past real capacity — an Operations failure, not a Fulfillment one. A Drop that's technically "live" but effectively inaccessible under demand, with no honest communication about what's happening. Silence during the highest-anxiety moment in this entire map.

**Recovery opportunities.** Real-time, honest capacity communication throughout the Drop, not only a final "sold out" message. A graceful, clearly-communicated waitlist or restock path for a fan who didn't make it in time.

## Mobile Purchase

**Emotional state.** Distracted, time-boxed. Happens between other things — a commute, a work break, a group chat — not in a dedicated shopping session, so patience for friction is close to zero.

**Goals.** Complete a purchase in the pockets of attention actually available, often triggered by something seen on the phone in the first place: a barkada group chat sharing a Drop, a livestream watched on mobile, a friend's post.

**Trust requirements.** The same trust as any purchase, with less tolerance for anything that takes extra time to verify. A fan in this mindset is more likely to simply abandon than dig for reassurance.

**Decision points.** Complete the purchase now in a short window, or lose the moment to distraction. Whether saved payment and address details are available to skip steps.

**Commerce opportunities.** Where the platform's mobile-first navigation priorities — Search and Favorites over a full category browse — pay off directly. The shorter the path from intent to completed purchase, the more of this journey's narrow attention window actually converts.

**Failure points.** Any step that assumes desktop patience: a long form, a slow-loading Product page, a checkout flow that doesn't fit the moment's short attention span.

**Recovery opportunities.** A cart and Wishlist that persist reliably across a session interruption, so a lost moment of attention doesn't have to mean a lost purchase entirely — the main safety net for a journey defined by limited time.

---

## Abandoned Cart

**Emotional state.** Hesitation that crept in somewhere between intent and completion — often price second-guessing, a distraction that broke focus, or a small unresolved doubt about sizing or legitimacy.

**Goals.** Not always fully formed. Sometimes this fan genuinely still wants the item and was just interrupted; sometimes the abandonment itself was the decision, quietly made.

**Trust requirements.** Whatever doubt caused the hesitation needs to be addressed directly, not papered over with a generic reminder — a fan who hesitated over sizing needs sizing reassurance, not a nudge that ignores why they left.

**Decision points.** Whether a follow-up resolves the original hesitation or simply repeats the same unresolved doubt. Whether the item is still available and at the same price by the time the fan reconsiders.

**Commerce opportunities.** An abandoned cart is a fan with proven, recent intent — the highest-value recovery opportunity on the platform per unit of effort, worth genuine investment rather than a single generic reminder.

**Failure points.** A recovery message that only restates "you forgot something" without addressing why. The item selling out, or a Drop ending, before the fan returns — turning hesitation into a permanently lost sale.

**Recovery opportunities.** A recovery Notification that speaks to the likely reason for hesitation where it can be inferred — a Confidence-scored size reassurance, a Trust reminder, a scarcity update if relevant. Reserving cart contents for a reasonable window during a Drop specifically, so ordinary hesitation isn't punished as harshly as pure first-come scarcity would.

## Returns

**Emotional state.** Disappointed, sometimes anxious about whether "official" and "trustworthy" were actually true. Every trust claim made earlier gets tested against reality here, and it carries more consequence for the platform's broader reputation than its size as a single transaction would suggest.

**Goals.** Get the situation resolved — a refund, an exchange, a replacement — without the process itself becoming a second disappointment layered on the first.

**Trust requirements.** The highest bar in this entire map. A platform whose whole premise is trustworthiness cannot be difficult to trust at exactly the moment something has already gone wrong.

**Decision points.** Request a refund or an exchange. Whether the return process is clear enough to attempt at all, or confusing enough to abandon and simply never return to the platform instead.

**Commerce opportunities.** A returns experience handled honestly and generously is one of the strongest trust-building moments available — a fan who has a good recovery experience often becomes more loyal than one who never had a problem, because the platform's character was actually tested and held up.

**Failure points.** A returns process opaque enough that the fan gives up and simply stops trusting the platform. Slow or unclear communication during the wait, which reads as hiding rather than resolving.

**Recovery opportunities.** Clear, proactive Notifications at every step, treated exactly as seriously as an original order's Fulfillment tracking. Treating a return caused by an authenticity or quality concern as a Trust-capability signal worth investigating at the Organization level, not just a single transaction to close out.

## What these thirteen journeys have in common

Group them by what they're actually asking of the platform, and a pattern falls out. **First Visit, International Fan,** and **Finding Team** are all asking "is this real, and is it for people like me?" — the same question, asked from three different starting distances. **Returning Fan, Buying Jersey,** and **Searching Player** are asking the platform to recognize an identity that's already formed and get out of the way of it. **Watching Broadcast** and **Mobile Purchase** are both fighting the same enemy — a narrow, fast-closing window of attention. **OFW** and **Gift Purchase** both require the platform to think about someone other than the person holding the phone. **Limited Drop, Abandoned Cart,** and **Returns** are the three moments where the platform's promises are tested hardest, and where silence is the single most damaging failure mode across this entire document.

Nearly every failure point in this map traces back to one of two root causes: trust being asked to do more work than it's actually earned yet, or urgency being met with silence instead of honest information. Every recovery opportunity in this document is, in one form or another, a fix for one of those two things.
